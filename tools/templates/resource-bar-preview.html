Resource Bars — visual preview

Every bar below is the real fragment shader from scripts/features/resource-bars/shader.mjs, compiled in this page's WebGL context and fed by the real animation model from anim.mjs. Nothing here is a mockup of the effect; it is the effect, with mock tokens instead of Foundry.

Health ladder

A 4-stop ramp interpolated in OKLab, so the green→yellow half never passes through olive. The bar is assembled from discrete plates with real gaps, so position reads as a second channel for health. Below 25% the stroke takes on the low-health pulse, the bloom turns hazard-red and a diagonal hatch fades into the fill — health encoded three ways that are not hue.

In motion

A value change is an event, not a length. The fill drops instantly and leaves a pale ghost that holds for 180ms then drains over 540ms; a shockwave expands from the point on the bar that moved, throwing spokes ahead of it; the whole quad takes a damped kick; the cap flashes; the readout punches and settles; and the delta floats off as a number you read rather than a magnitude you estimate. Healing runs the same machinery in green.

PF2e layers

Temp HP is a shield plate laid over the fill — nearly full height, translucent enough that the health underneath still reads, and carrying a scaled lattice that no other layer has. It never extends past the bar's end, so the scale cannot shift underneath the viewer. A raised shield gets its own hairline rail rather than stealing bar2, which a GM may legitimately have bound to something else.

Under a token

What it actually looks like in play, at true size on a 128px grid: hero bar, secondary rail, and — only while a shield is up — the shield rail.

Scale and zoom

The same 62% bar as the quad shrinks — a 128px grid down to a 44px one zoomed out. Bevel, segments and hatch fade out as each stops spanning a device pixel, rather than crawling between pixel centres. This is the uTexel contract from core/glsl.mjs, restated for an 8:1 quad.

Colour-blind ramp

The opt-in client setting: blue→cyan→yellow→orange abandons the red/green axis entirely and separates on lightness as well.

Live

Drag the slider to scrub, or hit the buttons to feel the timing. Hovering the bar arms the specular sweep — it is never on at rest, because forty of them at once is noise.
