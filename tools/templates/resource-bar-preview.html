Resource Bars — visual preview

Every bar below is the real fragment shader from scripts/features/resource-bars/shader.mjs, compiled in this page's WebGL context and fed by the real animation model from anim.mjs. Nothing here is a mockup of the effect; it is the effect, with mock tokens instead of Foundry.

Health ladder

A 4-stop ramp interpolated in OKLab, so the green→yellow half never passes through olive, sampled through a curve so red arrives while it still matters rather than in the last few percent. The bar is assembled from discrete plates with real gaps, so position reads as a second channel for health. Below 25% the fill goes to an arterial red no ramp stop reaches and takes on a slow breath — a ~4.6s loop with a drifting mottle over it, so the low state is legible without a pattern to look past. The second channel is temporal rather than spatial: the pulse is what you read when you cannot read the hue.

In motion

A value change is an event, not a length, and it is a sequence. The fill snaps to the new value and then everything freezes for 55ms — the hitstop is what makes the reaction read as a reaction rather than as the tail of a transition. On release: a wave crosses the whole bar in the direction the value moved — seen from directly above: a bowed crest line, a decaying train of crests behind it shaded by the slope of the surface rather than its height, and a three-stop colour ramp trailing the front, red down the bar on a hit and green out along it on a heal; the chip trail is cut white-hot and cools as it drains; a ring expands from the point that moved, throwing spokes and debris along the bar's axis; the readout counts to the new number in the colour of what happened; and the delta punches in at 1.35× before drifting off.

Nothing about the geometry moves. Not the frame, not the fill's height. No shake, no scale, no overshoot, no spring — every length here decelerates once and stops. Springs are the standard way to make a bar feel alive and on a bar they read as jelly; an instrument that wobbles is an instrument you stop trusting. The whole reaction is light travelling across something rigid.

The sequence, frozen

The same change, sampled at six points on its own timeline. Each cell is a real BarAnim advanced to that many milliseconds and then stopped, so the strip is the actual curve rather than a drawing of it. Read left to right: the freeze, the release, the front's travel, the trail's drain, the settle.

PF2e layers

Temp HP is a shield plate laid over the fill — nearly full height, translucent enough that the health underneath still reads, and carrying a scaled lattice that no other layer has. It never extends past the bar's end, so the scale cannot shift underneath the viewer. A raised shield gets its own hairline rail rather than stealing bar2, which a GM may legitimately have bound to something else.

Under a token

What it actually looks like in play, at true size on a 128px grid: hero bar, secondary rail, and — only while a shield is up — the shield rail.

Scale and zoom

The same 62% bar as the quad shrinks — a 128px grid down to a 44px one zoomed out. Bevel, segments and chevrons fade out as each stops spanning a device pixel, rather than crawling between pixel centres. This is the uTexel contract from core/glsl.mjs, restated for an 8:1 quad.

Colour-blind ramp

The opt-in client setting: blue→cyan→yellow→orange abandons the red/green axis entirely and separates on lightness as well.

Live

Drag the slider to scrub, or hit the buttons to feel the timing. Hovering the bar arms the specular sweep — it is never on at rest, because forty of them at once is noise.
