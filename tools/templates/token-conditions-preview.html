Token Condition Rail

The chosen direction, now carrying both halves of a PF2e creature's state: its conditions, and the effects its feats, items and spells applied. Every bar and every plate here is the shipped fragment shader — features/resource-bars/shader.mjs and features/token-conditions/shader.mjs, compiled in this page's WebGL context, sharing one HDR buffer and one bright-pass. Hover a scene to expand the plates.

At rest the plates are a packed block inside the token's own square — as many rows as the square has room for, wrapping into a second and third column. A creature in the sixth round of a real fight carries more than a single column can hold, and a column answers by growing down through the squares below it, over whatever is standing there. On hover the block unfolds into one labelled list hung off the flank, which is free to overlap its neighbours because it only exists while the cursor is on the token.

Conditions first, effects after, one wider gap between them — a column break in the packed block, a wider gap in the unfolded list. Position carries what kind of thing; tone carries how bad. Neither has to carry both.

Grid 64

The same rail at half the grid — the block is denser because a 64px square is denser, not because anything was re-tuned for it. Everything is derived from the grid, so the proportions hold and the detail that can no longer resolve leaves rather than turning to grain.

What an effect brings that a condition does not

A duration

The tone rail along the bottom of a plate is a hairline on a condition and a depleting gauge on anything timed. One element, two jobs — a second row of furniture on the most crowded part of the screen would cost more than it told you.

A sustain

A sustained effect draws its gauge in --gl-signal-pale. That is the suite's ceremony colour and it appears in exactly one place per feature; here it means somebody is spending an action every round to keep this alive, which is the one thing a countdown cannot say.

A secret

An unidentified effect is one the GM deliberately hid. The plate still appears — a creature visibly has something on it — but with no name, no artwork and no counter, and it says so with a hatch. A blank plate reads as a bug; a hatched one reads as withheld.

Somebody else's artwork

PF2e's condition art is a white silhouette; a spell's is a full-colour illustration, often on an opaque ground. Run through the silhouette path it would light the whole icon box as a solid square, so artwork is reduced to its luminance first and fifty item icons read as one set of sigils.

The tone system

PF2e ships no positive/negative flag — not on a condition, and not on an effect. Conditions are mapped by slug. Effects fall through a short table of traits that settle the matter on their own (curse, poison, healing) and then, failing that, to who applied it: self or an ally is a buff, the other side of the fight is not. That is a heuristic, it will be wrong sometimes, and so any single effect can be overridden with a flag rather than argued with.

Everything on this page is the module. The shaders, the animation models, the tone table and the plate proportions are imported from scripts/features/; only the sigils are stand-ins, because PF2e's own art lives inside a Foundry install. One plate below carries a full-colour stand-in instead of a silhouette, so the artwork path is exercised too.
